How it works

  1. 1

    Hold fn

    Put your cursor where the text should go: a message, an email, a doc, a prompt. Hold the fn key.

  2. 2

    Speak

    Talk the way you'd talk to a friend. Pause, ramble, correct yourself. The small bar at the bottom of your screen shows it's listening.

  3. 3

    Let go

    About half a second later the cleaned-up text is typed in. If there's no text box, Lipik shows it in a card so you can copy it.

Your voice never leaves your Mac.

Speech is turned into text by a model that runs on your Mac. The clean-up runs on your Mac too. Nothing is uploaded, there's no account, and it keeps working with Wi-Fi off.

  • Speech modelParakeet, running on your Mac's GPU
  • Clean-upQwen3 8B through Ollama, on your Mac
  • Your historySaved in a file on your Mac. Delete it any time.

Lipik is free and open source under the MIT licence. It's built on Handy by CJ Pais, a free dictation app, with a new interface, text clean-up, Hinglish support and meeting notes added on top.

Questions

What do I need?

A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). For the clean-up step, the free Ollama app with the Qwen3 8B model, about 5 GB. Without it, Lipik still types what you said, with simple fixes for punctuation and numbers.

Does it cost anything?

No. There's no subscription, no word limit and no account.

Does it work on Windows?

Not yet. Lipik is Mac-only for now.

How good is Hinglish?

It's in beta. English is the most accurate today. Hinglish works best when you speak clearly and close to the microphone, and it's improving with each release.

Why does macOS ask for Microphone and Accessibility?

Microphone so Lipik can hear you, and Accessibility so it can type into other apps and notice when you hold fn. Lipik doesn't send what it hears anywhere.

Can I change the key?

Yes. Pick any shortcut in Settings. Holding fn is the default because it doesn't get in the way of anything else.
